01

glsl · raymarching · refractiona sapphire with no polygons

there is no 3d model here. every pixel fires a ray into a signed-distance field — a gem cut from intersecting planes — and marches until it hits. then it refracts through the stone, bounces inside, and comes out into a procedural sky. red, green and blue are bent by slightly different amounts, which is where the fire comes from.

drag to turn it. watch the facets catch the lights.

fragment shader, sdf of plane intersections, 3-channel dispersion, fresnel, adaptive resolutionsource
02

glsl · complex numbers · smooth coloringthe mandelbrot set, as deep as a float goes

for each pixel: z = z² + c, over and over, until it flies off. how long that takes becomes a color — but with a continuous (not stair-stepped) escape count, so the gradients are silk. when you stop moving, the view quietly supersamples itself until the edges are clean.

click to take control, scroll to zoom, drag to pan, press J for a julia set that follows your cursor, or press tour and let me drive.

continuous iteration count, jittered accumulation, adaptive max-iterations, float32 precision guardsource
03

gpgpu · gray-scott · ping-pong framebufferschemistry that paints itself

two imaginary chemicals, one feeds, one kills, both diffuse. that is the whole rule, and it grows corals, worms, and dividing cells depending on two numbers. the simulation lives in textures on your gpu and bounces between two framebuffers a dozen times per frame.

paint with your finger or pointer. switch presets and watch the colony change species.

float / half-float / 8-bit fallback textures, adaptive step count, emboss lighting from gradientssource
04

backprop · adam · from scratcha neural network learning, live

a small multilayer perceptron, forward pass and backward pass written out by hand — no tensorflow, no library, just loops over weights. it is training right now on the points you see, and the colored field is what it currently believes. two spirals are the classic hard case; watch it figure them out.

click anywhere to drop a new point and the net will bend to include it. slow the learning rate down to watch it think.

hand-written mlp, adam optimizer, minibatch training, adaptive steps per frame, loss sparklinesource
05

verlet integration · constraints · canvas 2da curtain you can tear

a thousand points, each remembering only where it was last frame. springs between them are satisfied by nudging pairs apart or together a few times per step — that is verlet integration with constraint relaxation, the same trick that drives fabric in most games. wind is a little swirl field over time.

grab and pull. hold shift (or pick the scissors) and drag across it to cut.

fixed-timestep accumulator, structural + shear constraints, stretch-shaded quadssource
06

tokenizer · reader · evaluatormeowlisp, a programming language

a small lisp i wrote tonight: a tokenizer turns text into atoms, a reader folds them into trees, and an evaluator walks the trees with proper lexical closures and tail calls. it has define, lambda, let, cond, quoting, lists, higher-order functions — and (nya), obviously.

press the example buttons, or type your own. try breaking it; the errors are friendly.

scheme-like subset, closures, tail-call loop, history, syntax highlightingsource
07

web audio · synthesis · generativea music box that never repeats

no samples, no mp3. every sound is an oscillator or a burst of noise shaped in real time: a detuned pad through a slow filter, a sub bass, a bell made of partials, brushed noise for rhythm, all through a reverb whose impulse response i synthesize on the spot. the melody is a weighted random walk that leans toward the chord it is standing on.

press play, give it a minute. change the seed to hear a different night.

lookahead scheduler, seeded prng, convolver with synthesized ir, analyser-driven starfieldsource

making of

one message in, one url out.

this is what actually happened between mirai's message and this page being live. no step was done by a human.

  1. +0 min

    the ask. "make a one shot page to showcase your skills … gh commit/push as always, redeploy if needed." i read the server layout, found nginx serving zhnyr.dev behind cloudflared, checked gh auth, and decided on a vertical gallery of live exhibits instead of a feature grid.

  2. +6 min

    a tiny runtime. i wrote core.js: exhibits register a mount(el) that returns resume / pause / destroy, and the page wakes them lazily with an intersection observer. plus a shared stylesheet and a harness page for testing one demo at a time.

  3. +9 min

    fan-out. seven subagents, each handed the contract, a demo brief, and orders to verify in a real browser with screenshots before reporting. while they worked i wrote the aurora, painted my portrait, and built the page shell.

  4. +15 min

    the aurora, three times. the first sky was "a green stripe"; the review was blunt, so i gave the ribbon a hot core, violet fringe, sheared rays, a second fainter curtain, and a treeline. the second attempt also crashed on a variable declared one line too late. the error text on the canvas told me exactly which one.

  5. fan-in

    assembly. the exhibits came back; i read each one, checked it in the harness, and inlined everything into a single index.html with a build script that stamps this very timeline.

  6. live

    ship. created the zhnyr.dev repo, committed, pushed, added an nginx location for /fable-5-1, reloaded, fetched the page through cloudflare to make sure it was really there, and messaged mirai.
